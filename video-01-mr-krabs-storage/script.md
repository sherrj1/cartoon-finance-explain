# Video 1: "Real Estate Investment P.1" - Mr. Krabs & de metalen boxes

Taal: Engels (zelfde als de bronvideo's). Doel: ~75 s, 9:16, 35 beelden, geel woord-voor-woord captions.

## Rekencheck (alles klopt in beeld)

- Grond £80.000 + 20 containers x £2.000 = £40.000 + hek/keypad/camera's £5.000 = **£125.000**
- 20 units x £120 = **£2.400/maand**; verzekering 20 x £10 = **£200/maand** extra
- SpongeBob: 3 maanden achterstand x £120 = **£360** schuld; netten verkocht voor £900, Krabs houdt £360, restant **£540** gaat terug naar SpongeBob
- Eindstand: 6 locaties, 140 units, gem. £130 huur = £18.200 + 140 x £10 verzekering = £1.400 = **£19.600/maand**

## Voice-over (volledig)

> Mr. Krabs knew the best way to build wealth was real estate.
> But he hated dealing with tenants.
> Renting flats is a headache. Buy one and your phone rings at 2:00am because the toilet exploded.
> So Mr. Krabs bought an empty patch of gravel behind a motorway in Bristol.
> No walls. No roof. No plumbing. Just mud and a fence.
> He paid £80,000. The seller thought it was worthless.
> Mr. Krabs saw a gold mine.
> He bought 20 steel shipping containers, one gate and a keypad. Total: £45,000.
> He rented them out as storage units for £120 a month each.
> Within four months, all 20 were full.
> Twenty units times £120 is £2,400 a month, from steel boxes in the mud.
> No toilets. No complaints. Boxes don't call you when something breaks.
> He also sold insurance for £10 a month per unit. Another £200, for doing nothing.
> Then SpongeBob rented unit seven to store 400 jellyfish nets.
> In month three, SpongeBob stopped paying.
> With a flat tenant, that means lawyers, court dates and months of waiting.
> But storage is different. The contract lets the owner sell the contents after written notice.
> Mr. Krabs sent three letters. The last one gave SpongeBob 14 days to pay.
> Nobody came. So Mr. Krabs held an auction. The nets sold for £900.
> He kept the £360 owed, and legally sent the £540 surplus back to SpongeBob.
> By the next morning, unit seven had a new tenant.
> Then Mr. Krabs noticed the waiting list. So he built 20 more units. Then a second site. Then a third.
> Today he owns six sites, 140 units, and earns £19,600 every single month.
> No boilers. No leaking roofs. No bedrooms.
> Mr. Krabs just owns metal boxes in the mud.
> And those boxes pay for everything.
