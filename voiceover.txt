Mr. Krabs knew the best way to build wealth was real estate.
But he hated dealing with tenants.
Renting apartments is a headache.
Buy one, and your phone rings at two A.M., because the toilet exploded.
So Mr. Krabs bought an empty patch of gravel behind a highway outside Dallas.
No walls. No roof. No plumbing. Just mud and a fence.
He paid eighty thousand dollars.
The seller thought it was worthless.
Mr. Krabs saw a gold mine.
He bought twenty steel shipping containers,
one gate, and a keypad.
Total cost: forty-five thousand dollars.
He rented them out as storage units, for a hundred and twenty dollars a month each.
Within four months, all twenty were full.
Twenty units at a hundred and twenty dollars is twenty-four hundred dollars a month,
from steel boxes sitting in the mud.
No toilets. No complaints. Boxes don't call you when something breaks.
He also sold insurance for ten dollars a month, per unit.
Another two hundred dollars, for doing nothing.
Then SpongeBob rented unit seven, to store four hundred jellyfish nets.
In month three, SpongeBob stopped paying.
With an apartment tenant, that means lawyers, court dates, and months of waiting.
But storage is different. The contract lets the owner sell the contents after written notice.
Mr. Krabs sent three letters. The last one gave SpongeBob fourteen days to pay.
Nobody came.
So Mr. Krabs held an auction. The nets sold for nine hundred dollars.
He kept the three hundred sixty dollars owed, and sent the five hundred forty dollar surplus back to SpongeBob.
By the next morning, unit seven had a new tenant.
Then Mr. Krabs noticed the waiting list. So he built twenty more units.
Then a second site. Then a third.
Today he owns six sites, a hundred and forty units, and earns nineteen thousand six hundred dollars every single month.
No boilers. No leaking roofs. No bedrooms.
Mr. Krabs just owns metal boxes in the mud.
And those boxes pay for everything.
